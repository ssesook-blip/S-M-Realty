Sosúa — Cabarete — Puerto Plata

Your guide to buying on the North Coast.

What to expect, town by town, and how the purchase process actually works for foreign buyers in the Dominican Republic.

The North Coast — Sosúa, Cabarete, and Puerto Plata — is one of the few stretches of Caribbean coastline where foreign buyers can purchase property with the same ownership rights as Dominican citizens. There's no restriction on foreign ownership, no requirement to form a local company, and no residency requirement to close on a home. That's a big part of why the area has drawn a steady mix of retirees, remote workers, and investors for decades.

Each town has its own character and its own kind of buyer. Below is a quick guide to each, followed by how the purchase process itself works.

Area Guide

Sosua

Best for: beach life, walkability, and everyday convenience.

El Batey, the original commercial heart, is dense and walkable — narrow streets packed with restaurants, cafés, and beach bars within a few blocks of the sand. Los Charamicos, across the inlet, is the more residential, working-class Dominican side of town. Camino Libre, further out, is where most of the newer gated communities and condo developments have gone up.

Playa Sosúa sits inside a horseshoe-shaped bay protected by an offshore reef, keeping the water calm and clear — one of the better swimming and snorkeling spots on this stretch of coast. Playa Chiquita, a short drive east, is quieter, with a handful of cliffside restaurants overlooking the water.

Eating out: Casa Veintiuno (Casa 21), a boutique hotel restaurant in Reparto Tavares, is the top fine-dining pick in town — quiet, trendy, reservation-worthy. Fat Jesse's (4.8★, 157 reviews) does Southern American comfort food out of a small spot on Pedro Clisante; Dolci e Sapori Cake & Kitchen (4.7★, 434 reviews) is a higher-end Canadian-owned kitchen known for its breakfasts and desserts; Nona's Grill & Kitchen (4.6★) serves authentic Dominican food in a garden setting; and Margot, Bailee, and Restaurant Maria round out the everyday options, from pizza to oceanfront dining at Sosua Ocean Village.

Groceries and everyday errands: Supermercado Playero (4.5★, over 4,200 reviews) and Sirena Market (4.4★, over 2,500 reviews) are the two main supermarkets, both stocking imported goods alongside local produce. Banco Popular and Scotiabank both have branches and ATMs in the center of town.

Airport: Gregorio Luperón International (POP) is roughly a 10–15 minute drive.
Schools: The International School of Sosua, in El Batey, offers bilingual education from Pre-K through 12th grade.
Healthcare: Centro Médico Cabarete sits just outside town on the Sosua–Cabarete highway for everyday care; for anything more specialized, Hospital Bournigal in Puerto Plata is about 20 minutes away, and Santiago's larger hospitals (HOMS) are roughly 1.5–2 hours inland.

Area Guide

Cabarete

Best for: beachfront living, watersports, and strong short-term rental demand.

Cabarete is built around its beach and its wind. Cabarete Beach, right through the center of town, is the social hub — restaurants and bars line the sand with tables spilling right up to the water. Kite Beach, a short walk or drive further west, is a different pace entirely: longer, quieter, dominated by kitesurfers rather than sunbathers, with a wide-open, laid-back feel and its own strip of restaurants set back from the water.

Property ranges from beachfront condos right on the main strip to quieter villas in the hills above town with ocean views. It's a smaller, more concentrated market than Sosua, so well-priced inventory tends to move quickly, and short-term rental demand here is strong and tracks the wind season, which is why it draws a lot of buyers looking for an income property they'll also use themselves part of the year.

Eating out: La Kazba (4.9★, 141 reviews) is a Mediterranean spot run by owner Amal, cash-only and consistently rated one of the best meals in town; Restaurant Le Bistro (4.7★, 256 reviews) is a small French bistro tucked in an alleyway off the beach; Restaurant Tuvá (4.5★, 650 reviews) does Italian right on the water; and Vagamundo Coffee & Waffles (4.5★, over 1,600 reviews) is the go-to breakfast and coffee spot. Ultra Infinity, Viento, and Bliss round out the higher-end dinner options.

Groceries and everyday errands: Janet's Supermarket (4.4★, over 1,400 reviews) is the main full-size grocery store in town, with a smaller Janet's Plus location and Supermercado La Rosa as additional options. Banreservas and BHD both have ATMs in the center of town.

Airport: Gregorio Luperón International (POP) is roughly a 20-minute drive.
Schools: ISLA Academy, on the Playa Laguna campus, offers Pre-K through 12th grade on an American curriculum; ESD European School (Cabarete/Sosua) offers bilingual English/Spanish or French/Spanish instruction from Pre-K through Grade 12.
Healthcare: Centro Médico Cabarete (CMC), on the Sosua–Cabarete highway, is the main private hospital serving the town directly; Hospital Bournigal in Puerto Plata is about 30 minutes away for more specialized care.

Area Guide

Puerto Plata

Best for: value, year-round city living, and historic character.

Puerto Plata is the provincial capital and the largest, most functional city of the three — a genuine Dominican city center with Victorian-era architecture in its historic district, a working Malecón (boardwalk) along the water, and the region's main hospital, banks, and shopping all in one place. Prices per square meter here are generally the most accessible of the three towns.

Costambar and Playa Dorada, just outside the city center, offer a middle ground — gated, resort-adjacent communities with easy access to everything Puerto Plata proper offers, without being in the middle of the city itself.

Eating out: Casa Caribe (4.9★, 659 reviews) is a family-run spot known for fresh, well-prepared Dominican dishes and strong service; Los Tres Cocos (4.8★, 323 reviews), a bit outside the center in Coresi, is frequently cited as the best restaurant in Puerto Plata; Le Papillon (4.8★, 209 reviews), tucked away in Villas Cofresi, is the special-occasion, reservation-only pick; and Sambalu (4.8★, 353 reviews), right on the historic Parque Central, is worth it for the rooftop view over the plaza alone. Tío Pan (4.6★, over 2,400 reviews) is the reliable everyday spot for breakfast through dinner.

Groceries and everyday errands: Jumbo (4.5★, over 5,000 reviews) is the largest hypermarket in town, attached to a small mall with banking and other shops inside. La Sirena (4.4★, over 6,300 reviews) sits right on the Malecón, and José Luis Super Market (4.5★, over 1,800 reviews) is known locally for carrying harder-to-find international ingredients at fair prices. Scotiabank, BHD León, and Banreservas all have branches and ATMs in the city center.

Airport: Gregorio Luperón International (POP) is roughly a 15–20 minute drive from the city center.
Schools: Santa Rosa School, in Puerto Plata itself, is one of the oldest and most established schools in the city (Spanish-medium, with a number of foreign families enrolled); families wanting a fully English/bilingual international curriculum typically look to the International School of Sosua or Garden Kids International School, both a short drive away in Sosua.
Healthcare: Hospital Bournigal, in the city itself, is the main private hospital serving the entire North Coast region — this is the same hospital Sosua and Cabarete residents travel to for specialized care.

The Process

How a purchase works here.

The general sequence for a foreign buyer, start to finish.

1. Offer and reservation. Once you've found a property, a reservation agreement is signed and a deposit is paid to take it off the market while due diligence is completed.

2. Due diligence. A Dominican attorney verifies the title at the Title Registry Office (Registro de Títulos), confirms there are no liens or encumbrances, and checks that the property taxes are current.

3. Promise of sale. For most transactions, a Contrato de Promesa de Venta is signed, laying out the price, payment schedule, and closing timeline. A larger deposit is typically paid at this stage.

4. Closing. The final deed (Acto de Venta) is signed before a notary, the balance is paid, and the notary handles the transfer tax filing.

5. Title transfer. The Title Registry Office issues a new Certificado de Título in the buyer's name — this is the document that proves ownership going forward.
