Buying property in the Dominican Republic as a Canadian

We're a Canadian family from Ontario who moved to Sosúa in 2020. Here's what Canadians actually need to sort out before buying on the North Coast: provincial health coverage, the CRA, moving money, and how long you can stay.

By Sheena Sesook & Michael Galea · Updated November 2026

The short version

  • Canadians can own titled property in the Dominican Republic in their own name, with generally the same ownership rights as Dominican citizens. No visa or residency needed to buy.
  • Your provincial health plan has strict limits on time away, and it pays only a small part of emergency care abroad. Know your province's rule and buy travel medical insurance.
  • A DR property used mainly as your own vacation home generally doesn't go on Form T1135. A rental property generally does once your foreign property costs more than $100,000.
  • Canada and the Dominican Republic have a tax treaty. Talk to a cross-border accountant before you buy, not after.

Why so many Canadians buy on the North Coast

Sosúa, Cabarete and Puerto Plata have had established Canadian communities for decades, and it's easy to see why. Air Canada, WestJet and Air Transat fly direct from Toronto and Montreal to Puerto Plata (POP), mostly on winter schedules, and the flight is about four to four and a half hours. The airport is 10 to 20 minutes from all three towns.

Canadians don't need a visa to visit, and foreigners can buy and own property with generally the same rights as Dominican citizens. Prices on the North Coast are quoted in US dollars, and you can own a home in a gated community near the beach for a fraction of what a cottage costs back home.

We know the questions Canadian buyers have because we asked them ourselves. We moved our family here from Ontario in 2020, and we've been helping buyers through S&M Realty DR since 2021.

Keeping your provincial health coverage

This is the question we hear most from Canadians, and the answer depends on your province. Every province requires you to keep your main home there and limits how long you can be away. Go over the limit without making arrangements first and you can lose coverage.

ProvinceThe rule for time outside Canada
Ontario (OHIP)If you'll be away more than 7 months in any 12-month period, you need to arrange an extended absence with ServiceOntario before you leave. That can keep coverage for up to two years if you've been in Ontario at least 153 days in each of the two 12-month periods before leaving.
British Columbia (MSP)Contact Health Insurance BC if you'll be away 6 months or more in a calendar year. There's a 7-month vacationer allowance, and an extended absence of up to 24 months, available once every five years.
Alberta (AHCIP)Snowbirds on recurring vacations can be away up to 212 days in a 12-month period. Contact AHCIP before you leave and when you return. Extended absences for travel can go up to 24 months.
Quebec (RAMQ)You normally lose coverage if you're away 183 days or more in a calendar year. Absences under 21 days don't count, and once every 7 years you can be away longer for personal reasons.

Other provinces and territories have their own rules, so check with your health plan before you book a long stay.

Even within the limits, your provincial plan covers very little of the cost of care abroad. OHIP, for example, pays up to $50 a day for emergency outpatient care outside Canada and up to $400 a day for the highest level of hospital care. Ontario's own advice is that travel medical insurance is essential, and that's true whatever province you're from. Make sure your policy covers the full length of your stay and getting you home if you're sick.

What to tell the CRA

As long as you're a Canadian tax resident, Canada taxes your income from anywhere in the world, including income from a property in the Dominican Republic. Two things catch Canadian buyers out.

Form T1135 (foreign property)

You must file Form T1135 if the total cost of your "specified foreign property" was more than $100,000 at any time in the year. Whether your DR home counts depends on how you use it:

  • A vacation home used mainly by you is personal-use property and doesn't have to be reported. The CRA reads "mainly" as more than 50% personal use.
  • A rental property generally has to be reported. The CRA's own example is a condo rented out for eight months a year with a reasonable expectation of profit and used personally for four.

The penalties for not filing T1135 are significant, so if you plan to rent your property out, set this up with your accountant from the first year.

Rental income and selling

Rental income from your DR property is reported on your Canadian return. When you sell, any capital gain is reported in Canada too. The Dominican Republic also taxes capital gains (a flat 10% under Law 30-26, effective June 2026; see our closing costs and taxes guide), and Canada and the Dominican Republic have had a tax convention since 1976. Canadians can generally claim a foreign tax credit for income tax paid in the DR, so you aren't simply taxed twice in full. How that works for your situation is a question for a cross-border accountant.

Moving money from Canada

Most Canadians pay by international wire from their Canadian bank. Because prices are in US dollars, the exchange rate is part of your price. On a $300,000 purchase, a small difference in the rate your bank gives you can cost thousands, so compare your bank's rate with a currency-exchange service before you send a large payment.

Expect your bank, your Dominican attorney and the notary to ask where the money came from. Dominican anti-money-laundering rules require it, so keep your bank statements or sale documents handy. Only send money according to the payment schedule in your signed Promise of Sale, after your attorney has confirmed the title.

Visiting and how long you can stay

  • No visa needed for Canadian tourists.
  • Passport: the usual six-month validity rule is waived for Canadian tourists until December 31, 2026. Your passport just has to be valid for your whole stay.
  • E-Ticket: every traveller completes the free electronic entry and exit form at eticket.migracion.gob.do before checking in. One form covers up to 7 family members.
  • Staying longer than 30 days: you can, but you pay an extra fee when you leave. It rises with the length of your stay and can be paid online or at the airport.

If you want to stay longer: residency

You don't need residency to buy. If you plan to spend most of the year here, the Dominican Republic has residency programs that many Canadians use, including:

  • Retiree (pensionado): a lifetime pension of at least US$1,500 a month.
  • Independent income (rentista): at least US$2,000 a month in recurring passive income.
  • Investor: an investment of at least US$200,000, which can include real estate.

These thresholds change, so confirm them with a Dominican immigration attorney before you apply. Remember too that moving here for most of the year affects your provincial health coverage and can affect whether you're still a Canadian tax resident. Plan both sides of the move together.

Frequently asked questions

Can Canadians buy property in the Dominican Republic?

Yes. Canadians can own titled property in the Dominican Republic directly in their own name, with generally the same ownership rights as Dominican citizens. You don't need residency or a visa to buy.

How long can I stay in the Dominican Republic and keep my provincial health coverage?

It depends on your province. Ontario requires an extended-absence arrangement if you'll be away more than 7 months in a 12-month period. Quebec normally ends coverage at 183 days away in a calendar year. Alberta allows snowbirds up to 212 days in 12 months if you contact AHCIP. BC has a 7-month vacationer allowance. Always check with your own plan before a long stay, and buy travel medical insurance.

Do I have to report my Dominican property to the CRA on Form T1135?

A vacation home used mainly for your own personal use doesn't have to be reported. A rental property generally does if the total cost of your specified foreign property is more than $100,000 at any time in the year. Rental income and any gain when you sell are reported on your Canadian tax return.

Is there a tax treaty between Canada and the Dominican Republic?

Yes. Canada and the Dominican Republic signed a tax convention in 1976. Canadian residents can generally claim a foreign tax credit for income tax paid in the Dominican Republic. A cross-border accountant can tell you how it applies to you.

Are there direct flights from Canada to Puerto Plata?

Yes. Air Canada, WestJet and Air Transat fly direct from Toronto and Montreal to Puerto Plata (POP), mostly on winter schedules. The flight takes about four to four and a half hours.
